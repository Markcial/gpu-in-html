Wind tunnel

Twenty thousand particles of air, simulated on the GPU, blowing left to right across a page that is in the way.

Each element marked data-solid is measured with getBoundingClientRect() every frame and handed to the compute shader as a rounded box. The air is deflected twice by each one: the component of velocity heading into the surface is removed, which is what makes the flow slide along an edge instead of stopping dead at it, and a push down the outward normal keeps particles out of the interior.

Focus a field or hover it. The rectangle swells, and the wake behind it widens to match — the simulation is reading the same numbers the browser laid the page out with, so there is nothing to keep in step. Move the pointer into the stream: it is one more obstacle, and the only one you can steer.

The particles never travel back through the host. They are seeded once into a storage buffer, advanced by a compute dispatch, and drawn as instanced quads whose corners come from @builtin(vertex_index) and whose particle comes from @builtin(instance_index). No vertex buffer, no geometry, no readback.
