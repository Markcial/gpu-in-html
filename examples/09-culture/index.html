Culture

A Gray-Scott reaction growing on the page. Drag anywhere on the medium to inoculate it; focus a field below and a colony starts beside the card, level with it.

This is the first shader here that can see what it drew last time. The host keeps two identical rgba16float targets and swaps them each step: the reaction samples one and renders into the other, which becomes the input for the next call. Sixteen steps run per displayed frame, because a reaction advanced once per frame at sixty hertz is too slow to watch.

The precision is not incidental. A single step moves a concentration by less than one level of an eight-bit channel, so in rgba8unorm the whole system quantises to a standstill within a second of looking fine.

The page enters twice. Every rectangle marked data-solid is inert — the reaction is pinned at its fixed point inside, which is why the growth stops dead at this box instead of crawling over the words. And the distance to the nearest rectangle picks the feed and kill rates: close in, the culture goes labyrinthine; further out it breaks into spots that divide. A few thousandths is the whole difference, and the layout is what chooses.
